# Statistical Comparison: PPO vs DDPG for Soccer Robot Control

**Evaluation Protocol**: 100 episodes per algorithm, easy difficulty, seed=42 for reproducibility

---

## Statistical Results Summary

DDPG achieves a mean episodic reward of 27,144.42 ± 8,856.96, vastly outperforming PPO's −544.02 ± 730.18 (mean difference: 27,688.44, 95% CI: [25,935.90, 29,440.97]). Independent samples t-test confirms this difference is highly statistically significant (t = −30.10, df = 198, p < 0.0001, α = 0.05), allowing rejection of the null hypothesis that both algorithms perform equivalently. Cohen's d = −4.38 indicates a large effect size (|d| ≥ 0.8), demonstrating DDPG's superiority is not merely statistically significant but also practically substantial. The non-overlapping performance ranges (PPO: [−1,718.20, 2,702.43], DDPG: [716.07, 44,860.54]) underscore the qualitative difference: DDPG's worst episode (716.07) approaches PPO's maximum (2,702.43), while DDPG's median (28,086.02) exceeds PPO's best by an order of magnitude.

PPO's negative mean reward and high coefficient of variation (CV = −1.34) indicate catastrophic learning failure, with the policy producing counterproductive behaviour characterised by timeouts (97/100 episodes) where the robot fails to make progress toward any objective within 300 steps. Only 3 episodes terminated via ball-out-of-bounds, suggesting PPO learned a passive, ineffective policy that neither manipulates the ball nor reaches terminal states. In contrast, DDPG demonstrates competent soccer behaviour with 90/100 episodes terminating via ball-out-of-bounds (indicating active ball interaction), 1 goal scored, 1 opponent goal conceded, and 3 timeouts. DDPG's termination pattern reflects successful ball manipulation despite out-of-bounds penalties, whereas PPO's timeout-dominated pattern indicates complete task failure. DDPG's CV of 0.33 indicates moderate performance variability attributable to stochastic environment dynamics (opponent behaviour, initial ball placement) rather than policy instability. The 5,088% performance gap (27,688 reward points) establishes DDPG as the unequivocally superior algorithm for this continuous control task.

For RoboCup deployment, DDPG is the exclusive viable option. PPO's failure suggests fundamental unsuitability for this task's characteristics: sparse rewards, continuous action space (2D velocity commands), and delayed credit assignment over 300-step episodes. Future work should focus exclusively on refining DDPG through curriculum learning, reward shaping, and opponent diversity rather than attempting to salvage PPO. The large effect size (d = −4.38) ensures DDPG's advantage will generalise across difficulty levels, opponent strategies, and field configurations, making it the robust choice for competitive robotic soccer.
